# Automotive Diagnostic Workflow

## Why Diagnostic Process Matters

Professional diagnosis should identify the root cause of a problem instead of simply replacing parts.

---

## Step 1 – Verify the Complaint

Understand exactly what the customer is experiencing.

Check:

- Symptoms
- When the problem occurs
- Driving conditions
- Warning lights
- Recent repairs

---

## Step 2 – Visual Inspection

Before scanning or replacing parts, inspect:

- Wiring
- Connectors
- Fuses
- Hoses
- Fluid leaks
- Ground connections
- Physical damage

---

## Step 3 – Scan the Vehicle

Connect a diagnostic scanner and check:

- Stored DTCs
- Pending DTCs
- Permanent DTCs
- ECU information

Always record the codes before clearing them.

---

## Step 4 – Check Freeze Frame Data

Freeze frame data shows vehicle operating conditions when a fault was detected.

Common parameters include:

- Engine RPM
- Vehicle speed
- Coolant temperature
- Engine load
- Fuel trims
- Throttle position

---

## Step 5 – Analyze Live Data

Compare actual sensor values with expected operating conditions.

Examples:

- Engine Coolant Temperature
- O2 Sensor
- MAF/MAP Sensor
- Throttle Position
- Fuel Trims
- Engine RPM

---

## Step 6 – Test the Related Circuit

Depending on the fault, perform:

- Voltage test
- Resistance test
- Continuity test
- Ground test
- Signal test
- Component test

---

## Step 7 – Find the Root Cause

Do not stop at the first abnormal reading.

Determine why the fault occurred and identify the actual root cause.

---

## Step 8 – Repair and Verify

After repair:

1. Clear relevant DTCs if appropriate
2. Start and operate the vehicle
3. Check live data
4. Perform a road test
5. Re-scan the vehicle
6. Confirm the original complaint is resolved

---

## Diagnostic Principle

**Scan → Understand → Test → Find Root Cause → Repair → Verify**

A professional diagnosis fixes the cause, not just the symptom.
