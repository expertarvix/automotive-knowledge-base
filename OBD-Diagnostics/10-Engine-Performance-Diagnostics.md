# Engine Performance Diagnostics

## Introduction

Engine performance diagnosis is the process of identifying the root cause of problems such as poor acceleration, rough idle, misfire, hard starting, low power, excessive fuel consumption, or abnormal engine operation.

A professional diagnosis combines electronic data with mechanical and physical testing.

---

## Common Engine Performance Complaints

Typical complaints include:

- Hard starting
- Rough idle
- Engine hesitation
- Poor acceleration
- Loss of power
- Misfire
- Excessive fuel consumption
- Engine stalling
- Unstable RPM
- Check Engine Light

---

## The Four Basic Requirements of an Engine

A combustion engine generally requires four fundamental conditions:

1. Correct air supply
2. Correct fuel delivery
3. Correct ignition timing and spark
4. Adequate compression

A problem in any of these areas can affect engine performance.

---

## 1. Air Intake Diagnosis

Check the complete air intake system for:

- Air filter restriction
- Intake hose damage
- Vacuum leaks
- Throttle body condition
- MAF/MAP sensor operation
- PCV system problems

Unmetered air entering the engine can cause incorrect fuel mixture calculations.

---

## 2. Fuel System Diagnosis

Fuel delivery should be checked when symptoms indicate a possible fuel-related problem.

Possible checks include:

- Fuel pressure
- Fuel volume
- Injector operation
- Injector balance
- Fuel trims
- Fuel quality

Low fuel pressure can cause lean conditions, hesitation, poor acceleration, and loss of power.

---

## 3. Ignition System Diagnosis

The ignition system must provide a strong and correctly timed spark.

Check:

- Spark plugs
- Ignition coils
- Coil power supply
- Ground
- Wiring
- Ignition timing
- Misfire data

A weak ignition system can cause:

- Misfire
- Rough idle
- Poor acceleration
- Increased fuel consumption

---

## 4. Compression and Mechanical Condition

Electronic diagnosis cannot replace mechanical testing when an engine has a mechanical problem.

Possible checks include:

- Compression test
- Leak-down test
- Valve timing
- Camshaft timing
- Vacuum behaviour
- Cylinder condition

Low compression can cause persistent misfire, low power, and poor engine performance.

---

## Using Fuel Trim for Diagnosis

Fuel trims provide useful information about how the ECU is correcting the air-fuel mixture.

### Positive Fuel Trim

Positive correction means the ECU is adding fuel.

Possible causes include:

- Vacuum leak
- Low fuel pressure
- Restricted injector
- Unmetered air
- MAF under-reporting

### Negative Fuel Trim

Negative correction means the ECU is removing fuel.

Possible causes include:

- Excess fuel pressure
- Leaking injector
- Incorrect MAF reading
- Excess fuel delivery

Fuel trim must always be interpreted according to operating conditions.

---

## Misfire Diagnosis

A misfire occurs when combustion in one or more cylinders is incomplete or abnormal.

Possible causes include:

- Spark problem
- Fuel delivery problem
- Injector problem
- Low compression
- Valve problem
- Incorrect air-fuel mixture
- Timing problem

### Diagnostic Approach

1. Identify the affected cylinder.
2. Check misfire counters if available.
3. Inspect spark and ignition.
4. Check injector operation.
5. Check compression if required.
6. Compare relevant Live Data.
7. Identify the root cause.
8. Verify the repair.

---

## Engine Vacuum and Intake Behaviour

Intake manifold behaviour can provide useful information about engine operation.

Abnormal vacuum behaviour may be associated with:

- Vacuum leaks
- Valve timing problems
- Restricted exhaust
- Valve problems
- Mechanical engine faults

Vacuum data should be interpreted together with other diagnostic evidence.

---

## Diagnostic Workflow

For an engine performance complaint:

1. Verify the customer complaint.
2. Perform a visual inspection.
3. Scan for DTCs.
4. Save Freeze Frame Data.
5. Check Live Data.
6. Analyze fuel trims.
7. Check ignition and fuel systems.
8. Perform mechanical tests when required.
9. Identify the root cause.
10. Repair the vehicle.
11. Perform a road test.
12. Re-scan and verify the repair.

---

## Avoid Parts Guessing

Do not replace:

- MAF sensor
- O2 sensor
- Spark plugs
- Injectors
- Ignition coils
- Throttle body

only because a related symptom or DTC is present.

Test the system first and confirm the failure.

---

## Professional Diagnostic Principle

**Air + Fuel + Spark + Compression + Timing**

When diagnosing engine performance, evaluate these fundamentals systematically.

The goal is not simply to remove a warning light.

The goal is to restore correct engine operation and identify the root cause.

---

## Diagnostic Evidence

A strong engine diagnosis combines:

**Customer Complaint + DTC + Freeze Frame + Live Data + Fuel Trim + Electrical Testing + Mechanical Testing + Road Test**
